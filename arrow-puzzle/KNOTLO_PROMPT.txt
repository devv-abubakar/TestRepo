## 1. Core rules (engine, pure Kotlin, no Android imports, fully unit-tested)
- The board is a W×H grid. An **arrow** is a polyline of 2 or more orthogonally adjacent cells. Its last cell is the **head**, and the head points in the direction of the last segment (except Turn arrows, §3). Arrows never overlap.
- When the player taps an arrow, look at its **exit ray**: every cell from the head to the board edge in the head's direction.
  - If the ray is clear, the arrow slides out like a snake (the body follows the head along its path, then along the ray) and is removed. This counts as an **escape**.
  - If another arrow is on the ray, it is **blocked**. The arrow slides until it touches the blocker, turns red, and snaps back. The blocker shakes, the phone vibrates strongly, and the player loses 1 heart.
- The level is won when no arrows are left. It is lost when hearts reach 0.
- The player starts with 3 hearts (level 100 has 2). Stars are awarded as follows: 3★ for no mistakes and no hints, 2★ for at most 1 mistake, otherwise 1★.
- **Undo** restores the last escape, including the state of special arrows (free, up to 3 per level). **Hint** pulses one arrow that can move right now and its exit ray. The player starts with 3 hints, and each extra hint costs 40 coins.
- The engine is an immutable `GameState` with `tap(arrowId): TapResult` (Escaped / Blocked(blockerId) / Cracked / Locked / Won / Lost). This one engine powers every mode.

## 2. Level generator (pure Kotlin, deterministic, seeded)
- **Construction rule (this is what guarantees solvability):** place arrows one at a time. Each new arrow's exit ray must not cross any earlier arrow or its own body. Removing arrows in reverse placement order then always works. Store this order as the level's `solution`.
- **Placement:** go through mask cells in centre-first order (Chebyshev distance from the centre, normalised, plus random jitter of 0.15). For each free cell, try 16–26 random self-avoiding walks (continue straight with probability 0.55, length within the level's range) in both orientations. Shorten each walk from the head until its ray is clear, and require length ≥ 2.
- **Scoring each candidate:** `score = length + hardness*(14*blockedFreeArrows + 1.5*min(rayLen,12) - (rayLen<2 ? 40 : 0))`, where `blockedFreeArrows` is the number of earlier, still-free arrows whose exit ray this candidate covers. Keep the highest-scoring candidate. Make 3 passes so gaps get filled.
- **Verification:** simulate the stored `solution` with the real engine, special arrows included; it must win. Then compute metrics: the arrows free at the start, the number of rounds (repeatedly remove every free arrow), and the fill percentage. If a level misses its target metrics, retry with salted seeds (up to 30 attempts) and keep the best.
- **Shape DSL:** a pure-Kotlin vector DSL in a 100×120 design space.
  - Primitives: `circle`, `ellipse(rot)`, `rect`, `roundRect`, `polygon`, and `stroke(polyline,width)`, combined as `union` minus `cut` (holes for eyes, windows and similar). Test whether a point is inside with plain math.
  - Crop the shape to its bounding box, then sample each grid cell's centre to build the mask. The grid height follows the aspect ratio.
  - Every shape is made of named **parts** (for example `body`, `head`, `eye`, `tail`, `wing`, `fin`, `leg`, `flame`, `leaf`, `wheel`, `door`, `light`, `petal`, `sail`). Each part can carry a pivot point. The parts drive the living reveal (§4).
- Create **36 silhouettes** with cut-out details. Holes must be at least 2 cells wide at the grid width where the shape first appears.
  - Living (18): cat, fish, butterfly, bird, rabbit, turtle, elephant, owl, whale, snail, dog, horse, tree, flower, cactus, leaf, mushroom, octopus.
  - Non-living (18): house, car, rocket, cup, umbrella, key, star, anchor, bell, light bulb, airplane, sailboat, guitar, crown, clock, lighthouse, bicycle, castle.
- **Difficulty curve for 100 levels.** Level N uses seed `N*7919`. Every 10th level is a gold "Boss" level (+20% grid size, and it always includes the newest special arrow).

  | Levels | Board | Arrow length | Hardness | Special arrows |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1–3 | hand-made tutorial, 4×5 | 2–3 | – | – (1: 3 free straight arrows; 2: one arrow blocks another; 3: bent chain) |
  | 4–10 | rectangle 5×6 → 8×10 | 2–4 | 0 | – |
  | 11–25 | shapes, width 10–14 | 3–6 | 0.2 | Ice from level 16 |
  | 26–50 | width 14–20 | 3–9 | 0.4 | + Lock & Key from level 31 |
  | 51–75 | width 18–26 | 4–12 | 0.6 | + Turn from level 51 |
  | 76–95 | width 24–32 | 5–16 | 0.8 | all |
  | 96–99 | width 30–34 | 5–18 | 1.0 | all |
  | 100 | "The Knot", 30×40 | – | 1.0 | Best of 10 seeds |

  From level 11 on, 80% of levels are shapes, alternating between living and non-living, and each shape is used at least once. Special arrows make up 5–15% of a level's arrows.
- Generate levels on `Dispatchers.Default`, cache them, and pre-generate the next level.

## 3. Special arrows (each introduced with a 1-screen coach mark on first appearance)
- 🧊 **Ice:** drawn with a pale blue double outline. The first tap only cracks it (crack animation and sound, no heart lost, no movement). After that it behaves like a normal arrow. *Generator:* any arrow may be made Ice; the solution simply taps it twice.
- 🔑 **Lock & Key:** paired by a colour tag (up to 3 pairs per level). The lock arrow shows a padlock badge and cannot move until its key arrow (with a key badge) has escaped. Tapping a lock while it is still locked shakes it and shows "Locked: free its key first". No heart is lost. When the key escapes, the padlock pops open. *Generator:* the key must come before the lock in `solution`.
- 🔄 **Turn:** the head sits on a small dial. Its exit direction rotates 90° clockwise every time any other arrow escapes, and the head is redrawn on the dial with a 150 ms spin. When it escapes, the arrow follows its body, then turns into the dial direction. Allow at most 3 per level. *Generator:* convert an arrow at solution index r whose natural (clear) direction is d by setting its initial dial to `(d − r) mod 4`.
- The solution simulation in §2 must still win after all conversions. Unit-test each type.

## 4. Living reveal (signature feature)
When a shape level is won, the silhouette fills in with soft colours over 600 ms. Then it **comes alive** through part transforms (rotate around a pivot, translate, scaleY), with no libraries:
- **Idle loop (3 s):** blinking eyes (scaleY of the eye parts), a swaying tail, flapping wings or fins, flickering flames, swaying leaves, a lit lamp.
- **Exit act (1.5 s), defined for each shape:** the cat stretches and walks off, the fish swims away, the bird flies, the rocket launches with a flame, the car drives off with spinning wheels, the flower blooms, the lighthouse sweeps its beam, the clock hands spin, and so on.

Then the win sheet appears. Next to "Next Level" there is a **Share** button: it renders a 1080×1350 PNG card (the coloured shape, level number, stars and level code) and shares it through FileProvider with the Android share sheet.

**Zoo & Museum** (the Collection screen) has two tabs, Living (Zoo) and Non-living (Museum).
- Revealed shapes idle-animate in a grid. Tapping one plays its exit act in full screen.
- Unrevealed shapes show as "?" cards. Show progress such as "12/36".

## 5. Rendering and input (Compose Canvas)
- Draw arrows as paths with a stroke of 0.16×cell, round caps and joins, and a filled triangular head. Cache a `Path` for each arrow, and do not allocate memory in the draw phase.
- **Slide animation:** build an extended path (the body plus the ray, continuing 2 board-lengths off screen). Animate a distance `d` and draw the segment `[d, d+L]` using `PathMeasure.getSegment`. Duration is 220 ms plus 12 ms per cell, with FastOutSlowIn easing. Several arrows can animate at once.
- **Colours by state:**
  - Idle arrows: ink.
  - Escaping: blue.
  - Blocked: red flash.
  - Hint: a pulsing blue glow, with the ray drawn as dashed cells.
  - Cleared cells: faint dots.
  - Special arrows: their badges are drawn at the arrow's midpoint.
- **Zoom and pan:** pinch to zoom (1× to 4×) and pan, clamped to the board. Double-tap to reset. The Zoom button toggles between fit and 2.2× zoom.
- **Tap detection:** convert the touch to a cell through the transform. Tapping any cell of an arrow selects it. If the tap lands on an empty cell, pick the nearest arrow cell within 0.6 cell. Ignore taps on arrows that are animating.
- Confetti: 120 custom particles. The game must run at 60/120 fps on 30×40 boards; use `drawWithCache` and `Animatable`s that are read only in the draw phase.

## 6. Modes
1. **Journey:** the 100 levels in 10 chapters of 10 (Sprout, Meadow, River, Forest, Canyon, Storm, Glacier, Volcano, Cosmos, The Knot).
2. **Daily Challenge + Monthly Mosaic:**
   - Each day has a date-seeded shape level of medium-to-hard difficulty with special arrows. It pays 100 coins and builds a streak.
   - Each month has one original big illustration built with the shape DSL (12 scenes, one per month: mountain lake, night city skyline, ocean sunset, desert caravan, autumn forest, lighthouse coast, snowy village, hot-air balloons, river boats, starry camp, cherry garden, harbour). It is split into one tile per day.
   - Clearing a daily challenge reveals that day's tile with a flip animation. Missed days of the current month can be played later for 50 coins.
   - Completing the whole mosaic gives a badge in the profile plus 500 coins. Keep a gallery of past mosaics.
3. **Zen:**
   - Endless generated levels (shapes, hardness 0.3–0.5) with no hearts, no timer and no coins.
   - A blocked arrow just bumps softly, with no penalty.
   - A calmer palette and a soft synthesized ambient pad loop.
   - Count total arrows untangled.
4. **Duel (pass-and-play, 2 players, 1 phone):**
   - Players choose board size S, M or L.
   - Turns alternate: Player 1 is blue and Player 2 is orange. The active player's colour tints the turn banner and the tools.
   - Escaping an arrow scores points equal to its length and keeps the turn on a 3+ arrow streak; otherwise the turn passes.
   - A blocked arrow costs that player a heart (3 each) and passes the turn.
   - When the board is clear, the higher score wins. If a player loses all hearts, the other player wins.
   - Show a result screen with a rematch button. Hints and undo are disabled.
5. **Create (level editor + level codes):**
   - Draw a shape with your finger on a 24×30 grid, with brush sizes 1–3, an eraser, mirror symmetry, undo, and clear.
   - Starter templates are the 36 shapes, editable.
   - Choose a difficulty (Easy / Medium / Hard / Expert, which maps to hardness and lengths) and toggle special arrows.
   - **Generate** builds the level; if the mask is fewer than 30 cells, say "Draw a bigger shape".
   - Test-play it, then save it to **My Levels** (rename, delete).
   - **Level codes:** encode version, W, H, the mask as a run-length bitset, seed, difficulty and specials flags, plus a CRC-8, in Crockford Base32 with prefix `KN-` and a dash every 4 characters. Built-in levels have short codes (`KN-L042`, `KN-D20261031`).
   - **Share:** "Can you untangle my Knotlo puzzle? Code: KN-…".
   - **Enter code** dialog: auto-detect a code on the clipboard, then validate it, generate the level and play it. An invalid code shows a friendly error.
   - Unit-test the encode → decode → identical level round trip.
